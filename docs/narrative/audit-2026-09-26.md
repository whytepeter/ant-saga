## 1. Verdict

Level 1's dialogue is short, in voice, and mostly lands. The Opigo/Opumie double act works, and the "hundred of us / or one of him" setup and payoff is the best thing in the game so far. The trouble is around the dialogue. **Three things to fix first:**
1. **The sources disagree on the premise.** CLAUDE.md still calls the game SMALL GIANT (get home before sunset), and WORLD.md is still the Nigerian compound. Layout notes still point "home" at the back door.
2. **Amodu has no reason to follow the ants.** Nobody tells him why they came, what they want, or whether he can be made big again, and he never reacts to being 5 mm tall.
3. **The war doesn't add up across levels.** In Level 1 Akpuru's army marches overland to the colony gate and "the siege has begun". In Level 3 the termites can't cross the pond and the colony is untouched.

Reviewing the settled decisions (section 2b) adds one more S1: **nobody explains why the ants shrink their saviour** rather than recruit a giant, and the whole premise rests on that. Most other decisions hold up. The knife at M7 and the size of Level 1's survival kit are the two I'd change. Level 2 and Level 3 are sound in shape. Most remaining problems are timing (lines that play before their gameplay exists) and a handful of small facts.

## 2b. The settled decisions, reviewed

Each decision below was treated as settled. Here is each on its merits.

### F31 · S1 · Fact: why shrink the saviour at all?
**Where:** GAMEPLAY §1 ("a potion to shrink the hero of a prophecy"); STORY prologue; the draft Ch. 1–3.
**The problem:** a full-size human could stamp out Akpuru's army in a minute. No source says why the ants shrink the hero instead of asking a giant for help. A 12-year-old will ask this in the first scene, and the answer holds up the whole trio premise.
**The fix:** one line, early (a fit for F2's exchange in `start`):
`["Opumie", "Big, you'd crush the tunnels and half the colony. Small, you fit. That was the plan."]`
The prophecy already supports it: *stand tall where others crawl* means standing among them. It also sets up STORY's finale, where he has to get big again to stop the spray. **DECISION** only if the user has another reason in mind.

### F32 · S2 · Taste: the knife at M7 comes at the wrong moment (DECISION)
**Where:** GAMEPLAY M7; dialogue `knife`; gameplay_vision Note 3 (the knife is "Amodu's permanent tool").
**The problem:** a new weapon handed over mid-boss, with Opigo dangling, means the player learns its controls under the worst pressure in the level. It also repeats M3's beat (an ant hands him a weapon in a crisis). And the loadout note calls the knife his permanent *tool*, not a rescue prop.
**The fix:** recommended: Opumie gives him the knife at the **First Night** camp, as a tool for lashing and cutting fibre. That pays off Opigo's "can't even tie a knot" line. M7 then *uses* it (Opumie: *"Not the axe, you'll take his leg off! Your knife!"*). Alternative: keep M7 and give the knife a quiet training moment before the web. That costs more design and does less.

### F33 · S3 · Taste: the axe at M3 holds, with one addition
**Where:** GAMEPLAY M3; dialogue `axe`; level_story.gd `_give_axe`.
**The problem:** the plain handover is right, and the joke line is good. One gap: whose axe is it? If it's Opumie's own, he's unarmed for the rest of the game, and it will look like an oversight.
**The fix:** recommended, and still plain: the axe comes from the wrecked rest stop, a spare left there. That also gives the east detour a reason (F8). `axe` line 1 → `["Opumie", "Kid! Rest-stop spare. You can't fight them with your hands!"]`. Alternative: Opigo hands over his spare and grumbles about it. **DECISION** (small).

### F34 · S3 · Fact: "nothing is too heavy for an ant" holds
