# Real world at 5 mm: a cheat sheet

For catching the **obvious** mistakes a player would notice. This is an adventure with talking ants, not a documentary: realism never wins over fun. If only an entomologist would spot it, leave it, or list it once as a deliberate exception (bottom of this page).

## Ants
- **Strong:** a worker lifts roughly 10–50× its own weight. A team can drag things far bigger than one ant. So "an ant can't lift a pebble" is wrong unless the pebble is huge.
- **Climbers:** walk up walls, across ceilings and upside down on leaves.
- **Teamwork:** they work in numbers, form chains and bridges with their bodies, and carry food home in columns.
- **Senses:** mostly smell and touch (antennae, scent trails, vibration through the ground). Eyesight is poor, but for play our companions can still point things out.
- **Jaws:** chew leaves, seeds and silk; fight with bites and (some kinds) stings or acid.
- **Colonies:** a queen, workers and soldiers; nests underground or in wood; they farm aphids for honeydew.

## Amodu against the ants (the rule that keeps him special)
- He's about the same size as an ant, with a full-size boy's strength: in game, **lift ≤ 2.6 m, push ≤ 5.5 m** (object size).
- A strength task only works if **no team of ants could do it**: too heavy for the ants who can reach it, or too awkward to grip, wedged in a space where only a few ants fit, or something to throw, swing or lever. Ants drag; Amodu lifts, throws and shoves.
- **He leaps; ants don't.** A power jump takes him up to 12 m high and 11 m forward (about 7× his height), a running leap 6 m high. Ants climb and run but can't jump gaps, so leaps, throws and glides are his to use for them (all his moves: `world-facts.md`).
- Two legs and hands: he stands tall, grips, throws, swings tools, and uses human objects the way humans do (the **Know** thread).

## Other creatures
| Creature | What a player would expect |
|---|---|
| Spiders | feel vibration in the web; wrap prey in silk; orb-weavers wait in the web, wolf spiders hunt on the ground |
| Termites | pale, nearly blind, avoid light, build tubes and mounds from mud and chewed wood; ants are their enemies |
| Pill bugs (woodlice) | slow, armoured, roll into a ball when threatened; eat rotting plants |
| Ground beetles | fast night hunters, hard shells |
| Centipedes | fast predators with venomous claws; hide under stones and in damp dark |
| Grubs (beetle larvae) | soft, slow, live in soil and rotting wood |
| Praying mantis | still, camouflaged, strikes fast |
| Dragonflies | fast hunters over water |
| Aphids | tiny sap-drinkers; ants guard them for honeydew |
| Birds | the big danger from above for anything in the open |

## Life at 5 mm
- **Falls barely hurt:** small things hit a low top speed. A long drop is survivable; landing in water or a web is the real danger.
- **Water:** surface tension is strong. A dew drop is a drink; a puddle's skin can trap you. (Swimming in game is a deliberate exception; see below.)
- **Wind and rain** are violent: a raindrop is a falling boulder of water.
- **Heat and cold** matter more; small bodies warm and chill fast.
- **Sound:** humans can't hear a 5 mm voice. Big human sounds (footsteps, a door, a mower) are thunder.
- **Light:** a lit window or a torch is visible a long way across a dark garden.
- **Fire:** somebody has to light it. Glow-fungus gives light but no heat.

## Scale check (×360)
1 m in game = 2.8 mm real; 10 m = 2.8 cm; 100 m = 27.8 cm. A 1 cm pebble is 3.6 m. A pencil is a 68 m log. His school bag is a 150 m mountain. `docs/narrative/world-facts.md` has every place's size and position.

## Deliberate exceptions (keep them consistent)
- Insects talk, have names, families, jobs and jokes.
- The potion shrinks a human and keeps his full strength.
- Amodu can swim and paddle (no surface-tension trap for him, except in small puddles if a scene needs it).
- A seed puff can carry him (and the ants when the story needs it).
- Glow-fungus lamps give useful light.
- The termites are an organised empire with an army, generals and a queen.
- Story time is faster than real time (a mission takes minutes, a day passes in a level).
