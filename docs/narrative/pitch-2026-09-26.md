# Pitch: what Ant Kingdom Saga can be

A creative pitch from the narrative designer, 2026-09-26. It's built on the user's story (The Ugly One.pdf, STORY.md) and the canon (bible.md, GAMEPLAY.md). Everything here is a **proposal** for the user to accept or reject. Ideas I considered and cut are listed at the end of each section, with the reason.

---

## 1. The big idea

**You are the only one in the insect world who knows what things are.** Grounded and Smalland shrink you into a garden full of *resources*: a coin is scenery, a crisp packet is loot. In Ant Kingdom Saga the garden is full of *myths*. The ants have built a religion, a road network and a war around human junk they don't understand. Amodu is a mocked boy who has never been the expert at anything, and here he's the only one who knows the coin rolls, the zip is a lift, the hose is a pipe, and the giant who waters the flowers is his mum. His strength gets him respect; his *knowing* makes him the prophecy's "one of us, standing up". The story's theme (worth isn't appearance) becomes the core verb: the ugly kid sees what the beautiful ones can't.

### Signature mechanics and moments

**1. "I know what that is." (Human Sense)**
- **The moment:** at the Colony Gate, the ants kneel to the "Brass Moon". Amodu stands it on its edge and rolls it through the termite line.
- **Play:** certain human objects carry an ant legend (a subtitle when you look at them) and a real use only Amodu knows: roll the coin, pull the zip, twist the hose coupling, tip the bottle cap into a boat, use the marble as a lens to start a fire. Each is a puzzle answer made of knowledge, not a key.
- **Why it beats the others:** in Grounded an object is a material to harvest. Here it's a joke (the ants' name for it), a puzzle (its real use) and the theme, all at once.
- **Cost:** low to medium. Most objects are heavables with one scripted action. The ant names are a list of lines (§3, item 9).

**2. You play the weak ones first.**
- **The moment:** the cold open as Opigo, when a pebble is too heavy and you go around it. Twenty minutes later, as Amodu, you pick up that same pebble and throw it.
- **Play:** short playable scenes as an ant: the cold open (M1) and the Oyibo flashback (L2 Camp 2). Ant bodies climb walls but can't lift. You feel Amodu's strength by contrast, not from a number.
- **Why it beats the others:** it makes a power fantasy about *difference*, not size. It's the It Takes Two trick of swapping bodies, used for the story's theme.
- **Cost:** medium. Opigo already has a model and rig. The cold open needs a controller mode with lift disabled and wall-walking.

**3. The human day is the weather.**
- **The moment:** Mum crosses the lawn calling "Amodu!" Every step is an earthquake. He shouts back as loud as he can, and she can't hear him. Then she waters the flower bed.
- **Play:** the family's day moves on the day clock: Mum's search, the watering can's flood, the washing coming in, the back-door light at dusk. Each is a scripted event that changes the level for a while (a flood path, cover, a light to navigate by).
- **Why it beats the others:** Smalland's humans are absent; Grounded's are a mystery. Here the humans are his family, so every event hurts a little.
- **Cost:** medium. Scripted events on the day clock that reuse water and the camera shake. Only legs are shown (as already planned for the bully).

**4. The crowd is the scoreboard.**
- **The moment:** the resin boulder at the trials, lifted over his head. The laughter stops. Then the roar.
- **Play:** no reputation bar. Ants you pass change how they react: they point and whisper in Level 1, stare in Level 2, cheer in Level 3. Opigo's name for him changes the same way (mistake → kid → champion).
- **Why it beats the others:** most games show respect as a bar. Here it's the world and the words.
- **Cost:** low to medium. Reaction animations and line pools per level, and the dialogue pools already exist.

**5. ~~Smaller than the ants.~~ WITHDRAWN:** the user says Amodu is about the same size as the ants, so this idea is dropped.
- **The moment:** in the weaver ants' cell, the bars were woven for ants. Amodu is a little smaller. He slips through.
- **Play:** he fits gaps the ants don't (the reverse of the usual rule), and he holds doors, lifts and props for them. The two bodies are two halves of every puzzle.
- **Cost:** low. It reuses crawl and heave.

*Cut:* "Amodu's phone as a gadget" (a torch or map). It turns the phone into a tool, and the phone's value is emotional; it's kept as the message from Mum. *Cut:* "Ants follow scent trails you learn to read". It's nice, but it's a HUD feature, not a story one.

## 2. Levels 1–3, made better

What already works stays: the shut-gate / old-way / defend structure, "a hundred of us / or one of him", the web rescue, the coin, the resin pit.

### Level 1: The Road to the Kingdom

**1a. Mum's footsteps** (improves M6, The Door That Won't Move, in the Flower Bed)
- **The moment:** "Amodu! AMODU!" Her shadow over the daisies. He runs out into the open, waving and shouting. Opumie drags him back into the clover a second before her foot comes down. Then the watering can: the flower bed floods, and the daisy stair is the only way up.
- **Play:** a short scripted walk-through. Stay out from under the footfalls. Then the flood makes you take the daisy stair, so the climb has a reason.
- **Canon and theme:** it sets up his goal (be big again, get home) and the "Mum" phone message. Nobody can see him; that's the start of "stand tall".
- **Cost:** medium. Legs, a shadow, camera shake and a water plane in one area.

**1b. The ants' map of the gods** (a running gag across M2–M8, paid off at the gate)
- **The moment:** from the bag top (M4), Opumie names the garden: the Brass Moon (the coin), the Sky-Mother (Mum), the Yellow Log (the pencil), the Silver Tent (the crisp packet), the Endless Snake (the hose). Amodu gets every one of them wrong-then-right under his breath. At the gate, the Brass Moon rolls.
- **Play:** lines at the stages, plus look-at subtitles on legendary objects (signature 1). The payoff is the coin in M8, which is already planned.
- **Cost:** low. Lines only.
- **Self-critique:** it risks being a pile of jokes, so cap it: at most one new name per mission, and every name must pay off somewhere (see the setups table in bible §8).

**1c. The bridge the colony drops** (improves M8's ending)
- **The moment:** fleeing to the apple tree at dusk, looking back from the Great Root, you see the lolly stick tip and slap into the Rut. Hundreds of ants on its far end cut it loose, with termites still on it.
- **Play:** a camera beat in the run, no input. It shows the colony fighting, not only hiding (the kingdom was unseen until Level 3; audit F38).
- **Cost:** low to medium. One animated stick and a few crowds.

*Cut:* "Akpuru breaks Amodu's axe in M8". It's over-dramatising a simple prop, against the standing rule.

### Level 2: The Old Way

**2a. The riddles come back** (improves Camp 2, the Tale of Oyibo, and pays off in M6–M7)
- **The moment:** in the flashback as Opigo, Oyibo answers Ugo's riddles: a hole, a coin, an echo. Later in the Rootway, lost in the dark, Amodu shouts, and the echo shows him the way. At the Maw's crossing, *he* digs the hole (heave the boulder out of the pit) that the Maw falls into.
- **Play:** the three riddle answers become three verbs the player uses later: dig, flip, shout. Shouting is new and cheap: sound pings the tunnel.
- **Canon and theme:** it's straight from the user's Chapter 2, and it gives Oyibo's sacrifice a legacy in play. Legends adapt.
- **Cost:** medium. A shout that briefly lights the tunnel's shape. The rest reuses heave.

**2b. The cell woven for ants** (improves M4, Prisoners of the Canopy)
- **The moment:** the polite weaver guard: *"Your cell has been cleaned and prepared. Please enjoy your stay."* Amodu is the only one small enough to slip the weave. Opumie plans; Amodu complains loudly about the food *on purpose* to cover the noise. The user's joke, turned into the escape plan.
- **Play:** stealth out of the stitched-leaf barracks as the smallest one, freeing the ants from outside.
- **Cost:** low to medium. The barracks is the new canopy space already planned.

**2c. The painted wall's second figure** (improves M1, The Painted Wall; a reveal)
- **The moment:** the prophecy is painted: a two-legged figure among crawling ones. When the lamp reaches the far end, there's a *second* figure, a crawling one standing up beside him. Opigo goes quiet. Opumie: *"'One of us, standing up.' We read it wrong for a hundred years."*
- **Canon and theme:** it fixes the "why shrink him" logic in the lore itself, and it foreshadows Oyibo (a termite's son standing among ants). It doesn't explain anything yet.
- **Cost:** low. A texture and lines.
- **Self-critique:** it risks over-meaning. Keep it one image, one line, with no explanation until beyond Level 3.

### Level 3: The Kingdom

**3a. The smith is Opumie's ex-wife** (improves M2, The Trials: the axe upgrade)
- **The moment:** a whole game of ex-wife jokes, and the colony's master smith who upgrades Amodu's axe turns out to be her. She's lovely to Amodu and terrifying to Opumie. *"You still owe me a spear."*
- **Play:** the upgrade station (already planned) gets a character.
- **Canon and theme:** it pays off the user's running gag with a character, not cruelty. Family-friendly.
- **Cost:** low. An ant model reused, plus lines.

**3b. General Mba spars** (improves M2's third trial)
- **The moment:** the user's scarred general, who said "We need warriors, not myths", is the champion Amodu spars. He loses, and he's the first to salute.
- **Cost:** low. An ant model and a moveset.

**3c. Mercy is a lift** (improves M5, Akpuru; the choice)
- **The moment:** Akpuru sinking in the resin pit. The only way to save him is to heave him out, the heaviest thing Amodu has ever lifted, while the ants shout at him to stop.
- **Play:** the choice is a *verb*: heave him free (mercy), or walk away and let the colony's soldiers take him prisoner. Nobody is killed by a boy's hand.
- **Canon and theme:** stand tall where others crawl, literally lifting an enemy. The user's story wants mercy.
- **Cost:** low. A heave on a scripted prop.

*Cut:* "The pond freezes overnight". The summer setting is canon. *Cut:* "Titania is secretly a termite". It's a cheap twist that betrays the user's character.

## 3. The OPEN items (bible §10)

### 3.1 Can the Queen make him big again?
- **A. She can't. Only Ugo can.** At the end of the trials, Titania tells him the truth: the potion came from Ugo's pool; she doesn't know the way back. His goal moves to Ugo's cave, and Ugo's cave is where Oyibo's gear comes from.
- **B. She can, after the war.** She has the last dose to reverse it and gives it once the termites are beaten. That's clear, but it turns his help into a bargain.
- **C. She can, now, and he says no.** It's a strong character beat, but too early; nothing is earned yet.
- **Recommend A.** It joins his personal goal to the Oyibo mystery at the end of Level 3, so both threads point at the shed. He stays to fight because he now belongs, not because of a deal.

### 3.2 How did the ants get from Ugo's cave (under the shed) to the boxes?
- **A. Down the Endless Snake.** The cave's collapse broke into the water butt's overflow. They were washed down the hose and spat out of the leaking coupling onto the lawn (the Hose Run is already built). *"We came out of the snake's mouth."*
- **B. Under the shed door and across the vegetable patch.** Plain and walkable, but no story.
- **C. Carried off by a blackbird and dropped.** Funny, but a bird that's harmless once is a problem later.
- **Recommend A.** It uses the built hose, the coupling and the water butt, and it's a great line for the cold open or the idle pool.

### 3.3 Where is the termite Citadel?
- **A. The woodpile against the house (south-east).** The termites are eating the family's wood and the back step's frame. That's why Dad fills the insecticide sprayer (the user's finale), and why the termites come from the south-east.
- **B. The compost heap (far north-east).** It's in the backyard PDF and already on the skyline, but it contradicts every south-east line.
- **C. Next door, beyond the fence.** Mysterious, but it takes the finale out of the garden.
- **Recommend A.** It explains the direction of the war, motivates the finale, and keeps it all in one garden. The compost heap becomes the Forest of Decay (fumes as "illusions", no magic needed).

### 3.4 The mercy choice
- **A. Two real branches.** Mercy: Akpuru turns on the empire later (the user's story). Prisoner: he escapes, bitter, and Karubo leads the hunt.
- **B. Mercy only.** The choice is fake; the resin just holds him.
- **C. Mercy is the only action; walking away hands him to the soldiers** and closes the thread quietly.
- **Recommend A, kept light.** The branches meet again at the Citadel. Only who helps you at the gate changes: Akpuru, or nobody and Karubo in your way. Nobody dies by Amodu's hand.

### 3.5 The rest
- **Oyibo's second piece of gear:** (a) his helmet, with termite scent on it; (b) a riddle-stone from Ugo's cave; (c) his cloak pin. **Recommend (a).** Termite scent on a musketeer's helmet points toward "Him" among the termites without saying so.
- **The con beetle:** (a) at the Capstone's bead shrine, selling "holy dew"; (b) under the marble in the Flower Bed. **Recommend (a).** His map fragment marks the hidden missions: the phone, the mantis, the marble.
- **How many days:** **Recommend** Level 2 is one night and one day (Root Hall to the Last Camp), and Level 3 is three nights (the causeway grows a bit each night, which gives the war plan a clock).
- **The bully's name:** **recommend no name**, only legs and a phone. He stays a force of the human world until the finale. **General Mba in the trials:** yes (3b).
- **Ant names for human things:** Brass Moon (coin), Sky-Mother (Mum), Yellow Log (pencil), Silver Tent (crisp packet), Endless Snake (hose), the Capstone (bottle cap), the Glass World (marble), the Mountain (school bag), Ring of the Old Pot (planter ring), the Great Bridge (lolly stick), the Thunder-Bag (the bully's soda can).
- **The ex-wife payoff:** she's the colony smith (3a).

## 4. Beyond Level 3: the arc to the finale

**Act 2: The wide garden** (mined from the user's chapters 5–10)
- **Zina** lives in the pond's reeds, the last of a dragonfly village the termites burned there. She joins after a beetle ambush. She's a rival who teases him, then his closest friend. She gives the game flight (a mount, then a ride on her back).
- **The Forest of Decay** is the steaming compost heap. Its fumes cause the "illusions" (magic stays rare). Each hero sees what he wants: Amodu, a crowd that finds him handsome; Opigo, his glory; Opumie, gold turning into termites. Amodu breaks it because he knows fake kindness when he sees it.
- **Karubo** meets them at the fence line: Opigo's fight, his honour back.
- **The Hive of Madness** is a rotten fence post with shifting tunnels and King Craxus.

**Act 3: The Citadel** (the woodpile by the house)
- Disguised in mud and termite scent, they sneak inside. **Silkfang** ("You can't kill a shadow"): his blade halo weakens each time he attacks. **Queen Termina's** living chessboard: Opigo reads her strategy, Amodu tears out the pillar. Her last words: "You'll never defeat… him."
- **"Him" is Oyibo.** He survived by drinking from Ugo's pool under the shed. He learned he's the son of the termite priest who made the prophecy, and he means to end the war by ruling both kingdoms. Knowing everything, belonging nowhere: Amodu's dark mirror.

**The finale: "Stand Tall"**
- Dad has found termites eating the back step and fills the insecticide sprayer. The whole lawn will be drenched: the Citadel, and the colony with it.
- Only Ugo's knowledge can make Amodu big again, and Oyibo holds it. Amodu doesn't beat Oyibo; he reaches him. He is the boy who belongs nowhere too, and he chose the ants anyway. Oyibo gives up the way back.
- Amodu grows in the middle of the lawn, a boy again, standing tall. He stops Dad's arm and points him at the woodpile, not the grass. Then the bully comes round the side of the house, phone out. Amodu doesn't hide.
- **The last image:** Amodu lies flat on the grass, eye to eye with two old ants. Opigo: *"Kid."* He had to stand tall; now he's the one who gets down to their level.
- **Cost:** large, but it reuses the garden, the patio and the house wall. The only new space is the Citadel.

**Why it works:** every act pays something from Level 1. The phone is his mum. The hose is how the ants came. The coin: he knows human things. The mercy: Akpuru. The painted wall: "one of us, standing up". And "Him" is the third musketeer from the idle line "only two came back".
