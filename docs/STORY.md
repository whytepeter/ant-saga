# Story: Ant Kingdom Saga

Revised from the original 16-chapter draft. The characters, humour and big moments are kept. Sexual content is gone, the continuity is fixed, and every act is tied to a place in the compound so the story and the levels grow together.

**Setting:** a family compound in southern Nigeria. A painted bungalow with a veranda, a mango tree, red laterite earth, a patch of carpet grass, a small backyard farm (maize, cassava, pepper, ugu), a boys' quarters with the gutter behind it, the store, a termite mound in the far corner, and a block wall topped with broken bottles.

## Logline

A boy mocked for his face hides in his family's compound, gets shrunk to 5 mm by a botched ant rescue mission, and discovers that the last free insect kingdom needs exactly what the human world laughed at: someone who doesn't fit.

## Themes

- **Worth isn't appearance.** Amodu is mocked in both worlds. He earns respect by what he does, never by being "fixed". The mockery always pays off.
- **Legends adapt, legends die.** The musketeers are heroes past their prime, choosing whether to fade or matter one more time.
- **Community against conquest.** The ants win by cooperating. The termite empire wins by swallowing everyone into sameness, and its weapons (sirens, illusions, the Hive of Madness) all attack the heroes' insecurities.
- **"Stand tall where others crawl."** The prophecy's line is literal (a two-legged saviour) and personal (a boy who stops hiding).

## Tone rules

- Family-friendly (aim for a PEGI 12 / Teen rating at most). No sexual content, innuendo or seduction scenes.
- Comedy comes from character: Opigo's bravado, Opumie's sarcasm, Zina's teasing, the ants misreading human junk.
- Insults about Amodu's face are part of his arc. They must peak early and turn into respect; nobody gets a "free" cheap shot in the late game.
- Opumie's ex-wife jokes are a running gag. Use one per chapter at most, and give it a payoff (she shows up in Act 3).
- Magic is rare. The potion and the prophecy are the only true magic; everything else has a real-world explanation somewhere in the compound.

## Act 1: The Chosen One (Level 1: The Compound Grass, and the Colony)

### Prologue: The Endless War (cinematic)
- Termites overrun another colony; scarabs, centipedes and wasps already live under termite rule.
- In Titania's war council, General Mba wants warriors, not myths. Opumie argues legends inspire.
- Titania tells the prophecy: a termite priest who defected generations ago foretold a two-legged saviour from the human world, "flawed and unassuming", who would *stand tall where others crawl*.
- She sends the three musketeers: Opigo, Opumie and Oyibo.

### Chapter 1: The Shrew of Secrets (playable flashback, the Gutter)
*Merges the draft's Chapters 1 and 2, which ended the same way.*
- On the road: a comic escape from a praying mantis (Opigo trips over his own legs), and an old beetle cons them out of their food for a fake map.
- **Ugo's cave**, a shrew burrow off the gutter behind the boys' quarters: the Maze of Whispering Walls, the Webbed Chamber full of Ugo's spider "children", and three riddles:
  - What moves faster the more you take from it? A hole.
  - What has a head and a tail, is brown, and has no legs? A coin.
  - What speaks without a mouth and comes alive with wind? An echo.
- Ugo reveals the way to the chosen one, then wants the ants for his pantry. Oyibo collapses the tunnel on himself and Ugo so the others escape: "Keep going. The legend is bigger than all of us."
- Ugo's taunt plants the mystery: "Running from your own story, are you?"
- **No magic portal.** The escape tunnel runs up a drain pipe into the house: Opigo and Opumie climb out of the bathroom sink drain onto a cold white floor ("A giant ant hive?"), then out through the back door into the yard.

### Chapter 2: A Mix-Up of Epic Proportions (human-scale prologue in the compound)
- **Amodu at full size.** He hides in the grass behind the discarded cartons after another bad day at school. He drops his school bag, finishes an ice lolly and drops the stick, and trips over the hose from the overhead tank. *These become Level 1's landmarks once he is tiny.*
- **The bully arrives** with two friends, phone out, hunting for Amodu's hiding spot to film him for a laugh.
- **Playable ant stakeout (stealth-comedy stage).** The ants see the tall, confident bully and decide he must be the chosen one ("Look how shiny his hair is!"). The banter carries the scene: Opigo is scandalised by humans "rubbing faces" in a phone video; Opumie mocks him.
- **The mix-up.** The bully shakes a soda can to spray Amodu when he finds him. Just as Opigo uncorks the potion, the soda blast knocks the vial flying. Soda and potion fizz into a glowing green mist that drifts behind the boxes. Amodu sneezes and shrinks, keeping his full human strength.
- The bully sees glowing mist and "alien bugs" and runs off screaming. His prank started it all, and it is the first time Amodu is the one who isn't afraid.
- The ants stare at their saviour. Opigo: "No. No, no. That's a mistake." Opumie: "Give the kid a chance."

### Chapter 3: Into the Colony (Level 1 route A, then the colony interior)
- The ants lead Amodu across the compound grass: Backpack Hollow → Blade Forest → Dewdrop Garden → Capstone → Bare Patch → Colony Gate. This is the playable tutorial with the two ants as talking companions.
- **The human world keeps intruding.** His mother comes out to the veranda calling "Amodu!", then crosses the compound looking for him, and her footsteps shake the ground like earthquakes. He's right there and she can't see him. This sets the clock: he must get home.
- **The colony.** Ants whisper and mock him ("uglier than a crushed cockroach"). Titania doubts him. He trips bowing to her.
- **The trials:** moving platforms, lifting a resin boulder (the human-strength reveal) and a sparring match. The crowd turns from laughter to applause, and Titania names him champion.
- A messenger arrives: Akpuru is at the border.

### Chapter 4: The Termite Menace (Level 1 route B: Garden Patrol and the border)
- Amodu gets a thorn spear. The Eastern Tunnel defence and the fight at the Lolly-Stick Bridge.
- **Akpuru** duels Amodu and is too strong. Titania's pheromone call brings reinforcements. Amodu lures Akpuru into a hidden resin pit.
- Amodu spares him. Akpuru retreats, humiliated, and remembers the mercy (this pays off in Act 3).
- **Chapter boss: the Garden Wolf Spider** at Spider's Edge, one of Ugo's "children". It carries a fragment of Oyibo's gear, a first hint that Oyibo survived.

## Act 2: The Road to the Citadel (the backyard farm, the Gutter, the mango canopy)

*The draft reached the Citadel twice; in this version the heroes reach it once, at the end of the act.*

- **Chapter 5: Wings of the Last Village (the backyard farm).** A beetle ambush; Zina the dragonfly saves them and joins, and she scouts from the air. Her village was burned by termites, and she and Amodu bond over being the last of something.
- **Chapter 6: The Weaver Ant Army (the mango canopy).** Amodu gets them lost ("West is… this way!") and they climb into the mango tree, where polite, rigidly disciplined weaver ants arrest them in their stitched-leaf barracks. Zina gets them out by beating the guard captain at his own drill-rule quiz, while Amodu's complaining about the food nearly ruins it. Zina becomes the navigator.
- **Chapter 7: The Enchanted Forest of Decay (the rotting mango-leaf heap under the tree).** Illusions offer each hero what they want:
  - Amodu: a crowd that finds him handsome.
  - Opigo: his glory days.
  - Opumie: a treasury of gold that turns to termites.

  The Sirens of Decay are termite illusionists who speak to insecurities; no seduction. Amodu breaks the spell because he has seen people fake kindness before.
- **Chapter 8: Karubo (the edge of the farm).** Opigo faces the traitor who took his command and his wife, and wins back his honour.
- **Chapter 9: The Hive of Madness (a rotten wooden post under the boys' quarters).** Shifting tunnels, illusions and King Craxus the mad termite king. Amodu hurls chunks of hive wall, and Craxus falls into the larva pit.
- **Chapter 10: The Maw and the Swamp (the gutter).** The centipede guardian of the only crossing, then the stagnant gutter water full of mosquito larvae, where the Whisperers sing. Zina breaks their spell this time.
- The Citadel appears on the horizon: the termite mound in the far corner, with smoke rolling off the refuse heap beside it.

## Act 3: The Citadel (the termite mound, then the store)

- **Chapter 11: The Gate.** Akpombo the gatekeeper; Amodu snaps his resin spear mid-swing. The heroes go inside disguised in mud and termite scent.
- **Chapter 12: Silkfang.** The assassin duel. Silkfang's blades wear out the more he uses them, and Amodu spots the pattern. Silkfang falls in a collapsing cavern ("You can't kill a shadow") and later returns.
- **Chapter 13: The Queen's Gambit.** Queen Termina's living chessboard. Opigo reads her strategy; Amodu tears a pillar free and shatters the resin throne.
- **Chapter 14: Him.** Termina's last words: "You'll never defeat… him." **Him** is Oyibo. He survived the cave collapse by drinking from Ugo's Pool of Knowledge and learned the secret Ugo hinted at: he is the son of the termite priest who wrote the prophecy. Knowing everything and belonging nowhere, he now means to end the war by ruling both kingdoms. The legend Opigo and Opumie followed was his father's; the saviour it describes is a boy who never wanted to rule.
- **Chapter 15: The Store Raid.** Akpuru, remembering Amodu's mercy, turns against the empire and joins the heroes. The last battle is in the store, among cutlasses, kerosene and paint tins.
- **Finale: The Gardener's Shadow.** Amodu's father has had enough of termites eating the boys' quarters' door frames. He fills the insecticide sprayer to drench the termite mound and the whole compound grass, which would wipe out the ants too. Amodu must get back to human size (the potion reversed with Ugo's knowledge, which Oyibo gives up in the end) and stand up, literally, for the kingdom. He stops the spray, then faces the bully at full size, no longer hiding.

## Open decisions

- **Oyibo's name:** in Nigerian slang "oyibo" means "white person/foreigner". Keep it if the joke is intended, since it fits a character who turns out to belong to two worlds.
